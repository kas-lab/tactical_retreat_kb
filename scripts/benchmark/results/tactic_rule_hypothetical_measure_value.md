## Notable Findings

1. **Removing `isa effect` from NOT block** (V11): small consistent improvement
2. **Branch 1 reorder — links before isa** (V12): reduces candidate set before type check; marginal single-rule gain
3. **Combined V11+V12 = V16**: additive; best single-rule warm_avg in entire series
4. **Hoisting shared measurement (V13)**: TypeDB does NOT support constraints outside OR block at `fun` top level — parse error
5. **Named relation variable (V20 original)**: [REP1] error with `$var (...) isa T` syntax; FIXED with `$var isa T, links (...)` syntax — but fixed V20 still trails V16 (warm_avg 0.739ms vs 0.702ms)
6. **`isa!` (V15)**: neutral — no measurable gain (relative_effect/absolute_effect have no subtypes)
7. **@card(1..1) on relates measure (V14, V19)**: slight cold regression; marginal all-rules improvement; not worth schema change
8. **Absolute branch first (V17)**: large all-rules win (−14%) with no single-rule benefit; OR branch ordering affects multi-pair scan plans significantly

## Key Insight

Putting the **no-effect branch first** is counter-intuitive (the NOT subquery seems expensive) but wins decisively in the all-rules case. The 420-pair scan is dominated by pairs where `$tr` has NO effect on `$m` — the majority of tactic_rule × Measure combinations. By resolving those cheaply first, the planner avoids evaluating the absolute/relative branches for the bulk of inputs. The NOT subquery cost per-pair is much lower than previously assumed when it can prune early.
